Concept 01 · the teaching signal

Reward prediction error

Dopamine is not the pleasure. It's the surprise — the gap between the reward you expected and the reward you got. That gap is the signal the brain learns from.

Better than expected → a burst. Exactly as expected → nothing. Worse, or nothing at all → a dip below baseline.

Once a cue reliably predicts a reward, the dopamine burst walks backward in time onto the cue itself — the ping of your phone, the smell of the bar. The reward stops surprising you; the promise of it does the wanting. This is why anticipation can outshout the thing itself, and why cues drive craving long after the reward has gone flat.

Try each case and watch the firing trace.

Concept 02 · the executive

The prefrontal cortex, and its dopamine sweet spot

The prefrontal cortex is the part of you that holds a goal in mind, weighs the long game against the quick hit, and vetoes the impulse the reward circuit is pushing. It's the difference between wanting and choosing.

But it runs on a knife-edge of dopamine — an inverted-U. Too little and it can't hold a thought: apathy, blank focus, decisions deferred. Too much and it's noisy and scattered: everything feels urgent, nothing gets finished. It only works well in a narrow band in the middle.

Almost every state on this page shoves the PFC off its peak — which is why judgment and follow-through are the first things to go.

Concept 03 · use rewrites the wiring

Remodeling the mind after experience

Neurons that fire together, wire together. Every experience strengthens some synapses and lets others fade. Strengthening (long-term potentiation) grows new dendritic spines and studs them with receptors; disuse (long-term depression) shrinks spines until they're pruned away.

The mind you practice is the mind you become.

Repeat a state and you deepen its groove — a reward circuit fired again and again becomes easier to trigger (the physical basis of habit and craving), while a chronically stressed brain prunes the very connections that would calm it. This is also the way out: the same plasticity that carves the rut can carve a new path, given different, repeated experience.

Fire the synapse repeatedly to potentiate it — or leave it and watch it prune.
